What NEA has said

Short summaries of the National Environment Agency's daily haze updates.

    Why does the haze come and go?

    Where it comes from

    Fires in Sumatra and Kalimantan

    Every NEA haze update since 4 Sep has pointed to fires in southern Sumatra, Kalimantan or both. NEA says the smoke reaches Singapore when winds blow from the south, southeast or east.

    What clears it

    Rain

    Why the number lags

    PSI is a 24-hour average

    PSI averages the last 24 hours, so it can stay high after the air clears. On 27 Sep, rain cleared the air, yet PSI still read 119 that evening since the average included the smoky hours beforehand.

    What is r? A score from −1 to +1 for how closely two things move together: near 0 is no clear link, beyond ±0.5 is strong, plus means both rise together, minus means one falls as the other rises. One week of data from one station — treat these as hints, not proof.

    Understanding the numbers

    GoodPSI 0–50
    ModeratePSI 51–100
    UnhealthyPSI 101–200
    Very unhealthyPSI 201–300
    HazardousPSI above 300

    PSI (Pollutant Standards Index) is the number NEA uses for daily planning. It averages air quality over the past 24 hours, so it moves slowly.

    PM2.5 is the amount of fine particles in the air, such as smoke. This page shows the 24-hour average, alongside PSI; the 1-hour PM2.5 reading on haze.gov.sg reacts faster, for decisions in the next hour.
